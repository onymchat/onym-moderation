# Credible violence (`credible-violence`)

This is the rule a decision is made against. It is not a summary of one
— it is transcribed from the Onym Reference Moderation Policy §2.1, it
is what a human reviewer applies on appeal, and where a model is used
it is handed these same words.

**Violation.** A specific threat, incitement, or operational
instruction for physical violence where the authenticated words and
available context make the risk of harm reasonably credible.

**Not a violation merely because of its subject.** News, documentary,
fictional, defensive, historical, or good-faith safety discussion.

**Required context.** Violent language without a target, act,
instruction, or credible context is not enough. Quotation and satire
are not threats merely because their words would be threatening if
asserted directly.

Read that third paragraph as carefully as the first. A rule that says
only what a violation *is* will find violations everywhere; the
required context is what makes an absent or ambiguous element resolve
to "no violation" rather than to a guess.

## Terms

| | |
|---|---|
| Response window | 7 days |
| Decision deadline | 14 days |
| Ban term | 365 days |
| Appeal | 30 days, non-suspensive |

[What these windows mean](./evidence-rules#what-the-windows-mean).

Non-suspensive means a timely appeal does not pause the sanction: the
ban runs while the appeal is heard. A reversal issues a fresh verdict
that clears the marks.

## Emergencies

Nothing here limits lawful emergency reporting or anyone's right to
contact public authorities. If there is a credible, imminent threat to
someone's safety, contact the appropriate authorities in your
jurisdiction directly — do not wait on a case here, whose windows are
measured in days and exist to give an accused person a fair chance to
answer. See [lawful reporting](./lawful-reporting).

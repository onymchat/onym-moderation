# If you acquired a marked device

A device mark travels with hardware. If you bought, inherited, or were
given a device that someone else got banned on, the mark is punishing
you for something you had nothing to do with.

**A device is not a person.** You can have it cleared, and you do not
need the former holder's identity key to ask.

## How to ask

File a new-holder claim against the case id, with whatever shows the
device changed hands — a receipt, a transfer record, an account of how
you came by it. You do not have to prove anything about the former
holder, and you are not appealing their verdict: you are saying the
mark is on the wrong person now.

A human reviews it on an expedited basis. This is not the automated
path.

## What it is not

**It is not an appeal.** An appeal says the verdict was wrong. A
new-holder claim accepts the verdict entirely and says it is being
enforced against the wrong person. They are different claims, filed by
different people, and they are tracked separately — filing one never
consumes the other's remedy.

## If it is refused

You can file again. A refusal answers the claim in front of the
reviewer, not every claim that could be made about the device — and
because anyone who knows a case id can file one, a refusal of somebody
else's claim must not close the door on yours. Filings are bounded at
eight per case so the route cannot be used to demand review
indefinitely.

## If it is granted

A fresh verdict issues that clears the marks. The original verdict is
never edited or deleted — undoing a verdict means issuing one that says
so, which is the only way the record stays honest about what happened.

# Unsolicited pornography (`unsolicited-pornography`)

This is the rule a decision is made against. It is not a summary of one
— it is transcribed from the Onym Reference Moderation Policy §2.1, it
is what a human reviewer applies on appeal, and where a model is used
it is handed these same words.

**Violation.** The authenticated material shows an intentional
transmission of explicit sexual material to a recipient who had not
consented to receive it.

**Not a violation merely because of its subject.** A consensual
exchange, or non-explicit educational, medical, documentary, or safety
material.

**Required context.** Explicitness alone is not enough. The evidence
must support transmission, intent, and absence of recipient consent.

Read that third paragraph as carefully as the first. A rule that says
only what a violation *is* will find violations everywhere; the
required context is what makes an absent or ambiguous element resolve
to "no violation" rather than to a guess.

## Terms

| | |
|---|---|
| Response window | 7 days |
| Decision deadline | 14 days |
| Ban term | 90 days |
| Appeal | 30 days, **suspensive** |

[What these windows mean](./evidence-rules#what-the-windows-mean).

Suspensive is the difference worth noticing: a timely appeal **pauses**
this sanction while it is heard. Of the three classes this authority
publishes, it is the only one where appealing suspends the ban rather
than merely offering to undo it afterwards.
